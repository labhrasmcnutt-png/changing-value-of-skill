A labour-market lens for VET educators

The value of skill is being redistributed.

AI may not simply remove occupations. It can widen the distance between people doing the same job—amplifying a few, compressing the middle and changing what counts as expertise.

01 · Read the signal

From firm advantage to individual leverage

The article argues that technology, culture and scale are making exceptional individuals more economically consequential. Communication platforms enlarge audiences; AI labs chase scarce researchers; finance gives small teams vast computing power; and non-compete restrictions can increase mobility.

The workshop provocation

Are we preparing learners to enter an occupation—or to keep creating value as that occupation is reorganised?

Traditional workforce planning counts jobs. Educators must also examine how tasks, rewards, routes into expertise and bargaining power are changing within jobs.

03 · Redesign the learning promise

Six implications for VET

A narrow response—adding an AI tool demonstration—will not be enough. The curriculum, assessment and learner support model all need attention.

01

Teach task systems

Help learners see a job as a changing bundle of tasks: which are automated, augmented, newly valuable or disappearing?

02

Make judgement visible

Assess decisions, verification, trade-offs and responsibility—not only polished outputs that AI can produce cheaply.

03

Build complementary skill

Combine technical fluency with domain knowledge, communication, diagnosis, craft and the ability to work safely with others.

04

Protect routes to expertise

If entry-level tasks vanish, design simulations, coached practice and authentic placements that still build professional pattern recognition.

05

Teach leverage—and limits

Show how platforms, portfolios, networks and AI can extend reach, while examining precarity, dependence and unequal access.

06

Widen participation

Ensure tools, data, mentoring and opportunities do not become advantages available only to already-confident learners.

05 · Turn insight into action

A 35-minute workshop sequence

  1. 5 min

    Notice

    What surprised or concerned you in the evidence? Name one assumption it disrupts.

  2. 8 min

    Map an occupation

    Choose one role. Sort its tasks into automated, augmented, enduring and emerging.

  3. 8 min

    Find the missing middle

    Which entry-level tasks currently help novices become experts? What happens if those tasks shrink?

  4. 8 min

    Audit inclusion

    Who has access to the tools, coaching, networks and authentic practice needed to gain leverage?

  5. 6 min

    Commit

    Choose one curriculum, assessment or partnership change to test this term.

A balanced reading

Do not confuse stardom with a complete labour-market strategy.

Beware survivorship bias. Highly visible winners do not represent the whole workforce.

Teams and institutions still matter. Infrastructure, colleagues, trust and public systems make individual performance possible.

Technical skill is not the whole of value. Care, safety, reliability, tacit knowledge and collective capability remain essential.

VET has an equity role. Education should broaden access to agency and expertise—not merely identify a few “stars”.

Facilitator notes

Keep the discussion systemic

  • Ask “what changes inside the job?” before “will the job disappear?”
  • Challenge both technological determinism and complacency.
  • Look for lost entry routes into expertise.
  • End with a specific programme-level experiment.
